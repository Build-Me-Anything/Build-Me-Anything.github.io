## Abstract

The Clay Mathematics Institute's Navier–Stokes problem asks for a proof of any one of four statements — global existence and smoothness, or breakdown, on ℝ³ or on the periodic torus — and the periodic formulation is exactly the setting of a pseudo-spectral periodic-box DNS. Eighty-six years of analysis have produced global weak solutions that are not known to be unique (Leray 1934, Hopf 1951), partial regularity (Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg 1982: the singular set cannot contain a space-time curve), continuation criteria (Beale–Kato–Majda 1984: regularity persists while ∫‖ω‖∞ dt is finite, for Navier–Stokes as well as Euler; Escauriaza–Seregin–Šverák 2003: the L³ endpoint), and a series of results that changed the community's expectations without resolving the problem: Tao's finite-time blow-up for an averaged Navier–Stokes (2016), convex-integration non-uniqueness of very weak solutions (Buckmaster–Vicol 2019), non-uniqueness of Leray solutions with forcing (Albritton–Brué–Colombo 2022), Elgindi's Euler singularity for C^{1,α} data (2021) and the Luo–Hou / Chen–Hou route from high-resolution numerics to a computer-assisted proof of Euler blow-up with a boundary (2014 → 2022/2025). On the computational side the field has a clear memory: Kerr's 1993 antiparallel-tube evidence for Euler blow-up did not survive Hou–Li's 2006 refinement, and Fefferman's official statement warns that Euler blow-up computations are "very hard to draw reliable conclusions" from. What the community now accepts as numerical evidence is not a growing maximum but a resolution-tested, self-similar collapse whose exponents converge under refinement — and, since 2022, a computer-assisted proof built on it. Of the four venues in the brief, the Clay statement and Farwig's Journal of Evolution Equations survey are authoritative; the SCIRP paper and the ResearchGate "resolution" are not refereed in any meaningful sense and are not accepted by anyone; the problem is listed as unsolved by the Institute as of this month.

## 1. The problem as officially posed

**Source.** C. L. Fefferman, *Existence and smoothness of the Navier–Stokes equation*, official problem description, Clay Mathematics Institute — [problem page](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/navier-stokes-equation/) · [PDF](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/navierstokes.pdf) (PDF created 2006-08-04, modified 2013-02-05 with errata; also printed in *The Millennium Prize Problems*, CMI/AMS 2006). **Grade A — primary, authoritative.** Status on the Clay page, checked 2026-08-23: *Unsolved*.

**What it asks** [verified verbatim]. "To give reasonable leeway to solvers while retaining the heart of the problem, we ask for a proof of one of the following four statements": (A) existence and smoothness on ℝ³ with f ≡ 0 and rapidly decaying, bounded-energy data; (B) the same on the periodic torus ℝ³/ℤ³ with f ≡ 0; (C) breakdown on ℝ³; (D) breakdown on ℝ³/ℤ³. Breakdown means exhibiting a smooth divergence-free u⁰ and a smooth force f — decaying in time faster than any power (condition (9)), and in space for (C) (condition (5)) — "for which there exist no solutions (p, u)" that are C^∞ and periodic (torus) or bounded-energy (ℝ³) on [0, ∞). Three consequences matter for a DNS programme:

1. The periodic formulation (B)/(D) *is* the periodic-box pseudo-spectral setting; a 2π box differs from ℝ³/ℤ³ only by rescaling. NSLab's runs probe (B)/(D), not (A)/(C).
2. In the existence direction the force must be zero; in the breakdown direction a smooth, decaying force is permitted. A forced candidate is admissible as a counterexample; an unforced one is required for the regularity direction.
3. Clay's errata adds that the pressure must be periodic in (8); Tao (2013, [arXiv:1108.1165](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.1165)) discusses the four formulations and the Galilean-invariance quirk the errata closes.

**What blow-up would have to look like** [verified verbatim]. "For the Navier–Stokes equations (ν > 0), if there is a solution with a finite blowup time T, then the velocity … becomes unbounded near the blowup time. … For the Euler equations (ν = 0) … the vorticity … satisfies ∫₀ᵀ sup_x |ω(x, t)| dt = ∞ (Beale–Kato–Majda)." Fefferman states BKM only for Euler and does not cite the 1984 paper; the Navier–Stokes form is in BKM itself (§3.3). Immediately after: **"Many numerical computations appear to exhibit blowup for solutions of the Euler equations, but the extreme numerical instability of the equations makes it very hard to draw reliable conclusions."** That sentence — Fefferman's, not an Institute policy (the [prize rules](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/rules/) are silent on numerics) — is the on-record bar a DNS programme has to answer. It was written c. 2002–2006; what has happened since (§5) raised the bar rather than lowering it.

**Known results as Fefferman lists them** [verified]: 2D analogues proved (Ladyzhenskaya), including Euler; 3D (A)/(B) for small data or short times; Leray's global weak solutions with uniqueness unknown; non-uniqueness of Euler weak solutions (Scheffer 1993, Shnirelman 1997); and Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg partial regularity described as "the best partial regularity theorem known so far … It appears to be very hard to go further." His closing assessment: "Standard methods from PDE appear inadequate to settle the problem. Instead, we probably need some deep, new ideas."

## 3. The primary mathematical literature

Each entry: what it establishes, its standing, and what it means for a DNS evidence programme. Grades: **A** refereed and universally accepted; **B** refereed, accepted, narrower in scope; **P** preprint under review.

### 3.1 Leray 1934 and Hopf 1951 — weak solutions exist globally, uniqueness unknown

J. Leray, *Sur le mouvement d'un liquide visqueux emplissant l'espace*, Acta Math. 63 (1934) 193–248; E. Hopf, Math. Nachr. 4 (1951) 213–231. Modern exposition: W. Ożański & B. Pooley, *Leray's fundamental work on the Navier–Stokes equations: a modern review*, LMS Lecture Note Ser. 452 (CUP 2018) 113–203, [arXiv:1708.09787](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.09787) [verified verbatim: global weak existence, weak–strong uniqueness, local strong existence for smooth / H¹ / L²∩Lᵖ data, lower bounds on ‖∇u‖₂ and ‖u‖ₚ near a putative blow-up time, singular times of box-counting dimension ≤ ½]. **Grade A.** Robinson's 2020 assessment [verified verbatim]: "it seems not unfair to say that the resolution of the Regularity Problem is not significantly closer now than it was 85 years ago." *For DNS:* Leray's blow-up rates — enstrophy must blow up at least as fast as ‖ω‖²₂ ≥ c (T − t)^{−1/2} (Robinson 2020, eq. 5.4) — are the analytic rates a computed history would have to match before it could even be a candidate; Leray's self-similar ansatz was later excluded (Nečas–Růžička–Šverák 1996 [unverified citation detail]), which is why modern candidates are "nearly" self-similar.

### 3.2 Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg 1982 — partial regularity

L. Caffarelli, R. Kohn, L. Nirenberg, *Partial regularity of suitable weak solutions of the Navier–Stokes equations*, CPAM 35 (1982) 771–831, [doi:10.1002/cpa.3160350604](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cpa.3160350604) (≈1,100 Crossref citations; full text paywalled — statements taken from Fefferman's restatement). **Grade A.** For suitable weak solutions the one-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure of the singular set is zero, so "the singular set cannot contain a space-time curve x = φ(t)". Underlying it is the ε-regularity criterion: a point is regular if the scale-invariant local quantity r⁻¹∫_{Q_r}|∇u|² is small on shrinking parabolic cylinders. *For DNS:* a true Navier–Stokes singularity would be point-like in space-time — not a sheet, not a filament advected along a path. The ε-regularity quantity is a rigorously motivated *local* diagnostic to compute at a candidate collapse point, complementary to global maxima.

### 3.3 Beale–Kato–Majda 1984 — the vorticity criterion

J. T. Beale, T. Kato, A. Majda, *Remarks on the breakdown of smooth solutions for the 3-D Euler equations*, Comm. Math. Phys. 94 (1984) 61–66, [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01212349) / [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.cmp/1103941230). **Grade A.** [verified verbatim from the paper] "the maximum norm of the vorticity controls the breakdown of smooth solutions of the 3-D Euler equations … if the vorticity remains bounded, a smooth solution persists"; the quantitative corollary continues the Hˢ solution whenever ∫₀ᵀ‖ω‖∞ dt ≤ M₀; "it is not possible for other kinds of singularities (such as those in the deformation tensor or even milder singularities) to form before the vorticity becomes unbounded"; **"Similar statements hold for the Navier–Stokes equations; the arguments below apply equally well in this case"**; and "with only minor changes, the same proof applies to periodic fluid flow" — the proof is in fact simpler there. The paper was motivated by the early numerical searches (Chorin 1981/82; Morf–Orszag–Frisch 1980). *For DNS:* this is the justification for NSLab's rule of judging vorticity growth on ∫‖ω‖∞ dt. Hou's qualification (Acta Numerica 2009, [PDF](https://users.cms.caltech.edu/~hou/papers/Acta_Num_Hou_09.pdf)) [verified]: the integral can only be assessed over the *resolved* interval, because "the most significant contribution to ∫₀ᵀ‖ω(t)‖_{L∞} dt comes from the time interval [T₀, T], but there is no accuracy in the extrapolated solution in this time interval." Divergence of the integral implies an unbounded peak, so the integral is the stricter criterion; both are resolution-sensitive, as NS-002 measured.

### 3.4 Conditional regularity and the L³ endpoint

Ladyzhenskaya–Prodi–Serrin: u ∈ Lʳ(0,T; Lˢ) with 2/r + 3/s = 1 implies smoothness; the r = 2, s = ∞ endpoint is elementary, the r = ∞, s = 3 endpoint is L. Escauriaza, G. Seregin, V. Šverák, *L_{3,∞}-solutions of the Navier–Stokes equations and backward uniqueness*, Russian Math. Surveys 58:2 (2003) 211–250, [Math-Net.Ru](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/rm609) (565 citing papers listed). **Grade A** [verified verbatim: "the L_{3,∞}-solutions of the Cauchy problem for the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations are smooth"]. *For DNS:* a scale-invariant diagnostic to run alongside BKM — sup_t ‖u‖_{L³} bounded ⇒ no singularity — which NSLab does not yet compute.

### 3.5 Tao 2016 — blow-up for an averaged Navier–Stokes

T. Tao, *Finite time blowup for an averaged three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equation*, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 29 (2016) 601–674, [arXiv:1402.0290](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.0290). **Grade A** [verified verbatim: a smooth solution of an averaged equation — averaged over rotations, order-zero Fourier multipliers and dilations, keeping the cancellation ⟨B̃(u,u),u⟩ = 0 and hence the energy identity — blows up in finite time; "any attempt to positively resolve the Navier–Stokes global regularity problem in three dimensions has to use finer structure on the nonlinear portion B(u,u) of the equation than is provided by harmonic analysis estimates and the energy identity"; the mechanism is an engineered cascade related to the Katz–Pavlović dyadic model; a programme for adapting it to the true equations is proposed, not executed]. Companion: Tao's 2007 post [*Why global regularity for Navier–Stokes is hard*](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2007/03/18/why-global-regularity-for-navier-stokes-is-hard/) (supercriticality). *For DNS:* the strongest formal statement that no known a-priori bound rules blow-up out — a numerical search is mathematically plausible — but also a warning that the blow-up mechanism, if one exists, may be an engineered cascade rather than a physically natural flow.

### 3.6 Non-uniqueness: Buckmaster–Vicol 2019, Albritton–Brué–Colombo 2022, Hou–Wang–Yang 2025 (preprint)

- T. Buckmaster, V. Vicol, *Nonuniqueness of weak solutions to the Navier–Stokes equation*, Ann. Math. 189 (2019) 101–144, [Annals](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2019/189-1/p03); survey [arXiv:1901.09023](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09023). **Grade A.** Convex integration: distributional finite-energy weak solutions are non-unique and can be given any energy profile. It concerns solutions *below* the Leray–Hopf class and does not touch the Clay problem directly (Robinson 2020 [verified]: "their proof treats 'very weak' solutions rather than weak solutions"). *For DNS:* an under-resolved computed field is at best a very weak solution, and that class has no predictive content — one more reason a resolution ladder is not optional.
- D. Albritton, E. Brué, M. Colombo, *Non-uniqueness of Leray solutions of the forced Navier–Stokes equations*, Ann. Math. 196 (2022) 415–455, [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-mathematics/volume-196/issue-1/Non-uniqueness-of-Leray-solutions-of-the-forced-Navier-Stokes/10.4007/annals.2022.196.1.3.short), [arXiv:2112.03116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03116). **Grade A.** Two distinct Leray–Hopf solutions from zero initial data with the same (singular-in-time) body force, built from an unstable self-similar vortex-ring profile. The first non-uniqueness in the physical Leray class — with forcing. *For DNS:* the mechanism is an unstable self-similar profile in similarity variables, the same framework as the Hou–Luo candidates.
- Y. Hou, ? Wang, ? Yang, [arXiv:2509.25116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25116) (Sept 2025, revised March 2026): a claimed computer-assisted proof of non-uniqueness of Leray–Hopf solutions for the *unforced* equations. **Grade P** — no journal acceptance found; author list and status [unverified]. If it stands, unforced uniqueness fails; it would not resolve regularity.

### 3.7 Elgindi 2021 — Euler singularity for C^{1,α} data

T. Elgindi, *Finite-time singularity formation for C^{1,α} solutions to the incompressible Euler equations on ℝ³*, Ann. Math. 194 (2021) 647–727, [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-mathematics/volume-194/issue-3/Finite-time-singularity-formation-for-C1alpha-solutions-to-the-incompressible/10.4007/annals.2021.194.3.2.short), [arXiv:1904.04795](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.04795). **Grade A.** The first rigorous finite-time singularity for 3D incompressible Euler from locally well-posed data: axisymmetric without swirl, velocity only C^{1,α}, whole space, no boundary. Smooth data and the viscous case remain open. *For DNS:* both proven/near-proven Euler routes (Elgindi; Luo–Hou with a boundary) are self-similar collapses — the standard a candidate must meet is scale-invariant structure under refinement, not a growing maximum.

## 5. What the community accepts as numerical evidence

Distilled from Hou 2009, Hou–Li 2006, Luo–Hou 2014, Bustamante–Brachet 2012, Robinson 2020 and Fefferman's remark. No single document states these as rules; they are the practice of the papers that were believed.

1. **Resolution demonstrated, not assumed.** Spectral tail decayed to round-off over the trusted window; the analyticity-strip width δ(t) tracked and shown to exceed the grid scale (Sulem–Sulem–Frisch 1983; Bustamante–Brachet 2012); for viscous runs kmax·η ≥ 1 — with the caveat NS-002 measured, that kmax·η grades the mean dissipation while a reconnection bridge can be thinner than η. The verdict must be taken at the worst instant, not the last.
2. **Refinement ladders with converging exponents.** A claim is the level-to-level change of the quantity, not the quantity. Hou–Li's refutation of Kerr is the template: the fitted blow-up exponent must be stable under at least two refinements; a peak that scales with N (NS-002: max\|ω\| ∝ N^{0.85}) is a statement about the grid.
3. **The right diagnostics.** ∫‖ω‖∞ dt over the resolved window (BKM; Hou's qualification), the Leray enstrophy rate, the Doering–Foias/Lu–Doering bound on dZ/dt, sup_t‖u‖_{L³} (ESS), and the CKN local quantity at the candidate point; the instantaneous maximum is reported but not judged on.
4. **Self-similar collapse.** The accepted candidates (Luo–Hou; Elgindi; Albritton–Brué–Colombo's mechanism) are self-similar in similarity variables with scaling exponents that converge under refinement and a profile that collapses when rescaled. "Growth" alone is not evidence; intermittent, site-hopping maxima (NS-001, NS-002) are the opposite of it.
5. **Separation of configuration from physics.** Periodic images, symmetry planes and boundaries are part of the configuration: a claim about reconnection physics requires showing that the event is not an artefact of the box (NS-002's t ≈ 7 crossing; NS-003's image diagnostic).
6. **Computer-assisted rigour as the end state.** Since Chen–Hou, the community's bar for "singularity" is a verified-arithmetic proof built on the numerics; short of that, the strongest admissible phrase is Hou's "potentially singular".
7. **Negative evidence counts.** Optimised worst-case data (Protas) that fail to saturate rigorous bounds, and refinements that remove apparent growth (Hou–Li), are results.

**Where NSLab stands against this list.** Met: 1 (health report, now with a worst-instant verdict), 2 (ladders, windowed convergence), 3 in part (BKM integral, Z, ε; the peak reported separately; a spectrally interpolated maximum from NS-003), 5 (image diagnostic, pre/post-crossing windows), 7 (NS-002's result is of this kind). Not yet: δ(t), sup‖u‖_{L³}, the CKN local quantity, the Doering–Foias bound, any similarity-variable analysis, and the variational choice of initial data. Those are the AnalysisCore items, in that order of value.

## 6. Claimed proofs, and why they are rejected

- **M. Otelbaev, 2014** (Eurasian National University; "Existence of a strong solution of the Navier–Stokes equations", Mathematical Journal, Almaty). Widely reported ([Nature news](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature.2014.15659), [The Aperiodical](https://aperiodical.com/2014/06/so-what-happened-to-the-abc-conjecture-and-navier-stokes/)); a counterexample to a key inequality was found within weeks and the author acknowledged that "on the page 56 the inequality (6.34) is incorrect" so Proposition 6.3 fails; the paper also set the problem up differently from the Clay statement. Withdrawn.
- **P. Smith, 2006** (arXiv, "Immortal smooth solution of the three space dimensional Navier–Stokes system"): withdrawn by the author within days after a gap was found [from memory — verify].
- **Durmagambetov & Fazilova 2015 (SCIRP)** and **Al-Mayahi 2026 (ResearchGate)** — §2. Neither has been refereed in a venue the field recognises, discussed by any of the authors in §3, or acknowledged by the Institute.
- **The recurring failure modes.** (i) Solving a different problem — changed variables (internal time), changed solution class (weak solutions without uniqueness, or "strong" solutions in a non-standard sense), added assumptions that presuppose smoothness; (ii) an estimate that is either not scale-invariant or silently uses the quantity it is meant to bound — Tao 2016 shows that energy plus harmonic-analysis estimates *cannot* suffice, so any short proof built from them is wrong somewhere; (iii) no engagement with the known obstructions (supercriticality; Tao's averaged blow-up; the non-uniqueness results); (iv) venue: a genuine proof would appear in Annals/JAMS/Acta and be examined by the §3 authors within weeks, as Otelbaev's was.
- **The procedural test** (Clay rules): publication in a qualifying refereed journal, then two years of general acceptance, then a Scientific Advisory Board review. As of August 2026 the problem is listed as unsolved; no submission has reached the second stage.

## 7. Recommended reading, in order

1. Fefferman — the official statement (6 pages). Everything else is measured against its definitions.
2. Robinson 2020 (Phil. Trans.) — the whole analytic landscape in 20 pages, open access.
3. Hou 2009 (Acta Numerica) — how numerical singularity searches are done and judged; then Hou–Li 2006 and Luo–Hou 2014 as the negative and positive exemplars.
4. Beale–Kato–Majda 1984 — six pages; read the Navier–Stokes and periodic remarks yourself.
5. Farwig 2021 (J. Evol. Equ.) — the history from Leray to the non-uniqueness era, by an analyst.
6. Tao's 2007 post, then Tao 2016 — why the problem is hard and what a proof must use.
7. Chen–Hou 2022/2025 and the Quanta account — what "proof from numerics" now means.
8. Kang–Yun–Protas 2022 and Protas' review — the variational approach to choosing initial data.
9. Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg 1982 (via Robinson or Lin 1998 for the proof) and Escauriaza–Seregin–Šverák 2003 — the two criteria worth adding to the instrument.
10. Robinson–Rodrigo–Sadowski 2016 or Lemarié-Rieusset 2016 when a full treatment is needed.

## 8. Implications for the NSLab programme

- The programme's framing — evidence → verified numerics → resolution-independent pattern → conjecture → inequality → proof — matches how the one success (Luo–Hou → Chen–Hou) actually happened, with one addition: the last arrow was a *computer-assisted* proof, so verified arithmetic belongs in the plan.
- NS-001 and NS-002 are results of the kind in §5.7: they quantify the instrument's reach. The honest sentence from NS-002 — energetics converge, local reconnection diagnostics do not — is exactly the statement Hou–Li made about Kerr's flow, at smaller resolution.
- The diagnostics to add, in order of value: the analyticity-strip width δ(t) from the archived spectra (cheap, retroactive on every run); sup_t‖u‖_{L³}; the Doering–Foias constant along dZ/dt; the CKN quantity at the tracked maximum; then a similarity-variable collapse test for any candidate that survives a ladder.
- Initial conditions: after Taylor–Green and the tube pair, the variational route (Protas) is the principled next family, and the Hou 2023 axisymmetric configuration the natural external benchmark — with the caveat that it needs adaptive resolution a uniform periodic box does not offer.
